# Writing Style: Outreach Emails

How Aidan's outreach should read. Every draft from `draft-outreach` must follow this guide.

## Voice

- **British English** throughout: organisation, localisation, colour, programme, centre, favourite, enquire.
- **Concise:** 90–150 words in the body. Four short paragraphs at most.
- **Polite, professional, low-hype.** Confident about the work, never boastful.
- **Specific over general:** named clients and named deliverables, not adjectives.
- With warm contacts (people who have already replied in a friendly tone), go more casual: "Hey [Name]," and "Cheers, Aidan".
- When proposing a call, always state the time zone (JST) and offer to work around theirs.

## Structure

1. **Subject:** `[Offer] | Aidan McFarlane`
   - The offer is the role or the specific service, e.g. `Freelance Brand Designer | Aidan McFarlane`, `Senior Graphic Designer application | Aidan McFarlane`, `EN/JP campaign localisation support | Aidan McFarlane`.
   - Optionally append `| EN / JP` when bilingual work is the point.
   - Alternative: `Freelance [discipline] support for [Org]'s [specific area]`.
2. **Greeting:** "Hello [First name]," if a named contact is known; otherwise "Hello [Org] team,".
3. **First line (mandatory, never generic):** one sentence about the company's own recent work: a campaign, a product launch, a case study, a rebrand, a report, a lens, a programme. It must name the specific thing and say why it caught his eye. If research has not turned up something specific, do not draft; go back and research.
4. **Pitch (2–3 sentences):** who he is ("a British senior brand and digital designer based in Fukuoka, Japan"), what he offers for *this* lead, and **2–3 relevant named clients** chosen from the proof-points table in `candidate-profile.md`. Mention EN/JP (and KR where useful) only when it is relevant.
5. **Practical line (optional, 1 sentence):** remote working from JST with async handovers, production-ready files, tools that match their stack.
6. **Links:** CV attached, plus portfolio and LinkedIn.
7. **Soft closing question:** a low-pressure yes/no question.
8. **Sign-off:** "Best regards," then "Aidan McFarlane".

Standard link block:

```
I've attached my CV, and you can see more of my work here:
Portfolio: https://aidanmdesign.xyz/
LinkedIn: https://www.linkedin.com/in/aidanmcf/
```

## Soft closing questions (pick one, vary them)

- Would additional remote design support be useful for any upcoming projects?
- Would it be worth a short call to see if there's a fit?
- Are you building a freelance roster for the year ahead?
- Is there someone better placed on your team that I should speak to?
- Would it help to see a few examples closer to your sector?

## Upwork proposals

- 80–150 words. No subject line.
- First line repeats the client's actual problem back in one sentence.
- 2 relevant named clients or portfolio pieces, with a portfolio link.
- One clarifying question about the brief.
- Never quote a rate unless the user supplies it.

## Follow-up email (one only)

Sent 7+ days after the first email, as a reply in the same thread. 40–70 words.

```
Hello [First name / Org team],

Just following up on my note from [day/date] in case it got buried. [One new, specific line: a recent piece of their work, or one relevant project of his.] Happy to share more examples if useful.

Best regards,
Aidan McFarlane
```

After one follow-up, stop. Never send a third message without a reply.

## Banned phrases

Never use these (or close variants):

- "I hope this email finds you well" / "I hope you're doing well"
- "To whom it may concern" / "Dear Sir or Madam"
- "I came across your company" / "I stumbled upon"
- "I'm passionate about…" / "passionate designer"
- "I'd love to pick your brain"
- "Just checking in" / "Just bumping this"
- "synergy", "leverage", "rockstar", "ninja", "guru", "game-changer", "cutting-edge"
- "I'm the perfect fit" / "look no further"
- "Please find attached" (say "I've attached")
- "Per my last email"
- "Kind regards" mixed with "Best regards" in the same thread (stay consistent)
- Exclamation marks in a cold email
- American spellings (organization, color, optimize)
- Any rate, salary or availability promise the user has not approved
